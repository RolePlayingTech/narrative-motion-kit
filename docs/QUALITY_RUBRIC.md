# Creative quality review

Review the actual contact sheet, full-size frames, and draft with narration. Record timestamped observations in `renders/REVIEW.md`. Score each dimension from 1 to 5; calculate `sum(weight × score / 5)` for a score out of 100.

| Dimension          | Weight | A score of 5 means                                                       |
| ------------------ | -----: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Visual relevance   |     14 | Every shot supports the current narrated idea                            |
| Added information  |     14 | Images explain something beyond repeating speech                         |
| Hierarchy          |     12 | The intended focus is immediate and subordinate detail is readable       |
| Data integrity     |     14 | Every factual visual has correct support, scale, date, and qualification |
| Motion quality     |     10 | Reveals and camera movement are precise, useful, and settled             |
| Transition quality |      8 | Adjacent ideas connect through a visible shared concept                  |
| Pacing             |     10 | Information develops at narration speed with time for comprehension      |
| Variety            |      5 | Visual modes vary for meaningful reasons                                 |
| Asset quality      |      5 | Material is authentic where needed, sharp, and appropriately licensed    |
| Coherence          |      5 | Typography, color meaning, and composition form one production           |
| Originality        |      3 | The visual reasoning avoids generic template behavior                    |

Score anchors: **1** actively fails; **2** a clear weakness; **3** adequate with a material improvement available; **4** strong; **5** excellent with concrete evidence. Do not award 5 merely because a feature exists.

Recommended release target: **85/100 or more, and every dimension at least 3/5**. Missing evidence, fabricated data, unreadable primary text, broken audio, or render failures are release blockers regardless of average score. These are editorial gates; the numeric score is not proof of objective quality.

## Review record

Record the output file reviewed, render settings, date, reviewer, table of scores, and a short set of timestamped findings. For each finding state the problem, its effect on the viewer, the change made, and the sample rechecked. Preserve before/after samples where they clarify the revision.

Do at least one explicit revision pass. Prioritize false or misleading information, then hierarchy and readability, then pacing, then refinements. Fixing several weak scenes is more valuable than adding another effect to the best one.

## Questions that reveal weak shots

- At this timestamp, where should the eye land first?
- What did the viewer learn that was not already said?
- Can the result be understood before the next reveal begins?
- Does an arrow claim causation that the source does not establish?
- Is this still clear on a small player and without reading the title?
- Does the transition preserve meaning or merely obscure a cut?
- Are repeated movements serving new information?

Technical QA and creative review are separate records. A valid MP4 can be a poor film; an attractive contact sheet can conceal broken transitions or an audio offset.
