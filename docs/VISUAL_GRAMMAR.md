# Visual grammar for Świadek Dziejów

The default identity is serious, cinematic, and analytical: dark neutral grounds, warm paper/evidence surfaces, restrained accents, substantial typography, and deliberate empty space. Use theme tokens rather than inventing local palettes. The archive and technical themes change emphasis while preserving hierarchy. For narration-led historical productions, the implemented `chronicle` direction provides a dedicated palette, deterministic tactile surface and motion rules; read [HISTORY_STYLE.md](HISTORY_STYLE.md) before inventing topic-local historical styling.

## Hierarchy before decoration

One scene has one dominant thesis. The primary shape, face, number, or relationship should be identifiable within a glance. Support it with at most a few subordinate labels; the full source detail belongs in provenance. If three things are equally large, decide which one the narration needs now.

Build complexity in phases: establish the object, reveal its relation, then let the resulting pattern settle. De-emphasize information after it has served its purpose. Sources stay readable but visually subordinate. Small text is not an excuse to put an unreadable paragraph in frame.

## Motion has a job

Use motion to locate, reveal, compare, transfer, transform, or direct attention. A pan follows a mechanism; a push-in changes the level of evidence; a reveal follows the data's order. A static, readable hold can be more effective than perpetual drift.

Suggested development rhythm: hook 0.5–1.5 seconds, explanatory sequence 1–3 seconds, complex construction longer while meaningful information is still emerging. These are review heuristics, not automatic cut timers. Give viewers time to read the payoff.

## Typography

Use display typography for short theses and large numbers, and the readable body family for evidence and labels. Keep Polish diacritics in UTF-8. Prefer two short lines over shrinking a headline to fit. Use tabular numerals for changing quantities. Include units beside quantities and date ranges beside historical data.

Judge text at the smallest intended playback size and with video compression. A long word, source URL, or document title may need editorial shortening; automatic fitting has limits. Never reduce source labels until they are merely decorative marks.

## Documentary and illustrative material

Authentic photographs establish people, places, objects, and historical reality. Diagrams explain relationships photographs cannot show. Generated imagery can illustrate an impossible viewpoint or metaphor, but must not impersonate evidence. The same scene may combine both if their roles remain clear.

For political contrasts use comparable framing, scale, color treatment, expressions, and time on screen. Avoid a glowing hero against a desaturated adversary. Color encodes categories or data consistently, never unannounced moral judgment.

## Continuity and variation

Carry one visual property between shots: shape, position, scale, color category, geographic location, or narrative identity. A route can straighten into a chart line because both represent the movement being discussed. A fade is appropriate for time passage or a gentle reset; it is not a substitute for a conceptual connection.

Change visual mode when the reasoning changes. Do not alternate cards and counters just to fill time. Treat grain, vignette, camera shake, and overshoot as small emphasis controls. If an effect can be removed without losing meaning or material quality, consider removing it.

## Aspect ratio

Use normalized coordinates and safe layout helpers. Vertical output needs different hierarchy and fewer simultaneous objects. Review each scene in 9:16: shorten titles, stack comparisons, simplify labels, and use custom layout where necessary. A landscape composition being technically inside the viewport does not make it readable.
